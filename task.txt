Write a NASM program for Linux x86-64 (elf64, syscalls only, no libc) in one source file: an interactive temperament questionnaire for the terminal. Everything the program prints is in English, plain ASCII, no colours or cursor control.

Flow:
1. Print the title line "=== Temperament questionnaire ===", then a short explanation of three or four lines: the test has 12 yes/no questions, it scores two traits (extraversion and neuroticism, after Eysenck), and an answer is y or n followed by Enter (yes, no, 1 and 0 also work, in any letter case).
2. Ask the 12 questions below, one at a time. For each question print "Question N of 12", then the question text on its own line, then the prompt "Your answer (y/n): " without a newline, and read one line from standard input with the read syscall. The answers y, yes and 1 mean yes; n, no and 0 mean no; case does not matter. Any other line, an empty line or a line longer than 32 characters is not an answer: print "Please answer y or n." and ask the same question again without changing the question number. The program must not run past the end of its buffer on a long line and must not carry bytes of a previous line into the next one.
3. If standard input ends before all 12 questions are answered, print "Cancelled." on its own line and exit with code 1.
4. Scoring: every "yes" is one point for the trait the question belongs to. Odd-numbered questions measure extraversion (E), even-numbered questions measure neuroticism (N), six each, so each score is 0 to 6. A trait is "high" when its score is 4 or more.
5. After the last answer print exactly these lines, each ending with a newline:
   Result
   Extraversion: <E> of 6
   Neuroticism: <N> of 6
   Your temperament: <NAME>
   <DESCRIPTION>
   where <E> and <N> are the scores as decimal digits and NAME and DESCRIPTION come from this table:
   E high, N low  -> SANGUINE     "Sociable, lively and calm under pressure."
   E high, N high -> CHOLERIC     "Sociable, driven and quick to react."
   E low,  N low  -> PHLEGMATIC   "Reserved, steady and hard to rattle."
   E low,  N high -> MELANCHOLIC  "Reserved, thoughtful and prone to worry."
   Then exit with code 0.

The questions, in this order (E = extraversion, N = neuroticism):
 1 (E) Do you make new friends easily?
 2 (N) Do your moods change often?
 3 (E) Do you enjoy being the centre of attention at a party?
 4 (N) Do you often worry about things that might go wrong?
 5 (E) Do you like to talk to strangers?
 6 (N) Do you get upset easily?
 7 (E) Do you prefer plans with many people over plans alone?
 8 (N) Do you often feel tense or nervous?
 9 (E) Do you usually take the lead in a group?
10 (N) Do you often feel guilty without a clear reason?
11 (E) Do you feel full of energy most of the day?
12 (N) Do small problems keep you awake at night?

The program reads only standard input and writes only standard output, so a test can feed it the answer lines and compare what it prints and its exit code. Return one complete source file in a single code block.
